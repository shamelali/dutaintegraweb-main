PDPA for SMEs: the 2024 amendment, minus the legalese

Duta Integra Solutions · Cyberjaya · 7 min read · Not legal advice — confirm specifics with your counsel.

Malaysia's Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (PDPA) was amended in 2024, and the changes matter for companies that used to assume "PDPA is a big-corp problem". If you collect customer names, phone numbers or IDs — which is every SME with a contact form — the amended regime touches you.

What actually changed

  • Mandatory breach notification. When personal data in your control is breached and it is likely to cause significant harm, you must notify the Commissioner, and in serious cases notify the affected individuals too. "We'll keep it quiet and fix it quietly" is no longer an option.
  • Data protection officers (DPOs). Organisations must appoint a DPO and publish their business contact details. For an SME this can be a founder or ops lead with a defined mandate — it does not need to be a new hire, but it does need to be someone accountable.
  • Broader scope. The amendments tighten rules around data processors (the vendors who host your systems or process data for you) so you cannot outsource your responsibility by contract alone.
  • Higher stakes. Penalty ceilings were raised significantly, and offences now attract penalties calculated against turnover for bodies corporate in certain cases.

What a typical 10-person company should do about it

  1. Map your data. List where customer PII lives: website forms, WhatsApp Business, email, spreadsheets, accounting software, cloud drives. You cannot protect what you have not listed.
  2. Name a DPO. Put their name and contact on your privacy notice. Give them two hours a month and real authority to ask "why do we still have this spreadsheet?"
  3. Fix consent at collection. Your website forms need a plain-language purpose statement ("We use your details to reply to this enquiry") and a link to a privacy policy that matches reality.
  4. Write a one-page breach playbook. Who detects, who decides, who calls whom within 72 hours. Rehearse it once a year like a fire drill.
  5. Lock down access. Shared logins are where breaches start. One account per person, MFA on email and admin panels, and revoke access the day staff leave.
  6. Check your vendors. Hosting, CRM, payroll — ask each one where data is stored and what happens on breach. Put the answers in writing.

The mindset shift

Treat PDPA like safety equipment, not paperwork. The goal is not perfect compliance theatre; it is being able to say honestly: we know what data we hold, only the right people can reach it, and if something goes wrong we will detect it, contain it and tell the regulator fast.

SMEs that can demonstrate data discipline win enterprise deals faster than those waving generic certificates.
